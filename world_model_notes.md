## Summary of where the discussion landed

1. **Movement is room to room**, not continuous coordinates.
2. **The open world has one uniform density.** Every density change happens at a seam the story explains: a gate, a dock, a cave mouth, or entering an explored layer.
3. **The overworld grid is an S2-style cube-sphere quadtree**, about level 15 (~281 m cells), behind an engine grid interface so small worlds can use a flat grid.
4. **Every overworld cell can have one dense layer:** generated by `explore`, patched, replaced by a hand-built area, or part of a multi-cell anchored area.
5. **Dense layers are islands** (they don't stitch to each other). You leave by walking off the edge or through a defined exit, and land in the overworld cell in that direction.
6. **`explore` enters at the centre.** Encounters place sides by perception: the winner picks the spacing, ties and group-vs-group get neutral placement.
7. **Spacing is resolved deterministically**, from a preset (player or NPC creator) or derived from signals.
8. **A battle-grade explore layer is about S2 +4 levels** (16×16 squares of ~18 m), configurable per project.

## 1. Movement model: why one uniform density

Variable density bands in the open world (fine cells near roads and towns, coarse in the wilderness) were considered and rejected. Where a coarse cell borders fine cells, one edge touches several rooms, and these problems follow:

| Problem | What the player sees |
|---|---|
| Moves don't reverse | `east` then `west` lands you in a different room from the one you started in (at 4:1 density, three times out of four) |
| Loops don't close | Walking a square (`n e s w`) across the seam ends one room off. Hand maps and automappers tear. This is the accidental non-Euclidean geometry |
| Distance per command changes | One move covers 1 km on one side and 250 m on the other. Speedwalkers route through coarse cells; chases cover uneven ground |
| Maps get awkward | Character-grid maps must draw coarse cells as blocks |
| IDs aren't stable | If new GIS data subdivides a cell, its ID disappears and every override, delta and saved location keyed to it is orphaned |

Fixes exist (hidden true position, capping the ratio at 2:1) but each one keeps some of these problems or brings continuous coordinates back underneath. Uniform density removes all of them. The only geometric oddity left is "bigger on the inside" at anchored areas, which is deliberate and which players have accepted in Diku-family games for decades.

## 2. The overworld grid

### Grid as an engine interface

The engine defines a grid interface: cell key, neighbour by direction, cell centre and outline in lat/lon, parent, children. A game picks an implementation in config.

| Implementation | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cube-sphere quadtree (S2 style)** | Planets | Leaning. Near-equal cells (about 2× area spread across the planet), hierarchical keys |
| **Flat local grid** | Small worlds, islands, hand-drawn fictional maps | True squares, no distortion |
| Equal-angle lat/lon | Optional regional grid aligned to GIS rasters | Cells narrow toward the poles (250 m wide at the equator, 125 m at 60°, 43 m at 80°); collapses at the poles |

Rejected for the overworld: lat/lon with fewer columns toward the poles (brings back density seams in rings around the planet), UTM-style projected zones (hard seams every 6° of longitude), hexes/H3 (six directions don't fit Diku's eight compass exits; twelve pentagons).

This is what makes "very small to a whole planet" a config choice instead of a rewrite.

### Defects and how they're handled

No square grid covers a sphere without irregular points. On a cube-sphere there are eight, at the cube corners, where a cell has 7 neighbours instead of 8.

- **Corner placement:** with stock S2 orientation, three corners fall on or near land (see the facts table). The engine applies a fixed rotation to coordinates before every S2 lookup and picks a rotation that puts all eight corners in open ocean. The rotation is part of the world config and is **fixed forever** once any IDs exist, because changing it moves every cell. A fictional planet chooses its own.
- **Exit names:** across a cube face edge the grid's own axes rotate. Exit labels (n, ne, e...) come from the true bearing to each neighbour's centre, not from grid axes.

### Cell size

| Cell | Moves to cross 10 km | Land cells (Earth) | All cells (Earth) | What fits in a cell |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 m | 100 | 14.9 billion | 51 billion | A farmstead, a pond |
| **~250 to 281 m (S2 L15)** | **~36 to 40** | **~2.4 billion** | **~8.2 billion** | A hamlet, a field, a river crossing |
| 500 m | 20 | 596 million | 2 billion | A village, a hill |
| 1 km | 10 | 149 million | 510 million | A town, a valley floor |

The counts use an even 250 m square for simplicity; S2 L15 cells average ~281 m, which lowers the counts by about a fifth.

Pros of ~250 m: ~70 elevation samples per cell at 30 m data (honest slope and aspect); a 50 to 100 m river is a feature inside a cell rather than a band of water rooms; roads curve believably; travel feels like walking.

Cons: Earth's circumference is roughly 160,000 moves. Neighbouring cells risk reading alike, which is the description generator's job and partly the dense layer's.

### Cell size versus data resolution

These are two separate knobs.

1. **Cell size is a pacing choice:** one uniform value for the world.
2. **Data fills cells at whatever resolution it comes in.** The bake resamples every layer onto the cell grid:
   - **Finer than the cell** is summarised: dominant land cover, mean and maximum elevation, slope, aspect, which roads and rivers cross the cell and through which edges.
   - **Coarser than the cell** (open sea, poorly mapped regions) is interpolated, plus procedural detail seeded by cell key (the Worldbuilder "procedural detail as texture" idea).

Data quality changes how rich a cell's metadata is, never its size. Better data later improves cells in place and IDs never move. If cell size followed data resolution, density seams would reappear wherever data quality changes (state borders, survey edges, coasts).

### Planet-scale data volume

Pre-baking every cell is not viable at planet scale: a 16-byte record for each of ~8 billion cells is about 130 GB. Instead:

- The bake stores source layers as compressed tiles at native resolution.
- Cell metadata is computed from tiles on demand and cached.
- Tiles load lazily, like rooms.
- Regions without detailed data fall back to a coarse global layer plus procedural detail.

### Per-cell metadata drives content

The metadata record per cell (biome, elevation band, slope, water, roads and rivers by edge, nearest settlement, region ID) feeds:

- **Descriptions:** fragment pools chosen by biome and features, seeded by cell key plus season, so they're stable and change only with the season.
- **Exits:** blocked by cliffs and deep water using slope and elevation thresholds.
- **Spawns:** tables keyed by biome and region, deterministic from cell key and time.
- **Builders:** patches target cells by coordinates; tools query by metadata ("every forest cell within 2 km of a road") to place content in bulk.

### Long-distance travel

At ~250 m, large worlds need a travel primitive in the engine: route along roads, rivers or sea lanes with pathfinding, game time passing, and interruption for encounters or weather.

## 3. Dense layers

### One dense layer per overworld cell

Every overworld cell can have at most one dense layer. Its source follows the existing override precedence, lowest to highest:

1. **Generated** (what `explore` creates)
2. **Patched** generated layer (a builder adjusts some sub-rooms)
3. **Replacement** (a hand-built bridge, shrine or hamlet on one cell)
4. **Multi-cell anchored area** (a city on, say, a 3×3 footprint)

A town and an explored meadow are the same kind of object; one is hand-built. `explore` in a town cell enters the town; in a wild cell it generates.

### Anchored areas

- **Footprint:** the area claims whole overworld cells. You can't walk across a footprint on the overworld grid; you go in or around.
- **Fills whole cells:** if the content is smaller than its footprint (a bridge on a 281 m cell), the builder either adds filler rooms or blocks the empty squares with a described reason ("the riverbank drops away").
- **Entrances:** as many as the builder wants, each mapping an overworld cell and direction to an inner room.
- **Own frame:** its own room scale, layout and z-levels (cellars, towers, mines). Distances inside are free.
- **The one geometric promise:** exits land you on the matching side of the footprint. In the west gate, across town, out the east gate, and you're on the east edge.
- **Location-aware:** a geographic anchor point, so rooms inside inherit weather, daylight, season and region state from their footprint cells.

The same mechanism scales down to a two-room shrine on a single cell, so "dense" is a per-place decision, not a world property.

### Terminology

Avoid "instance" for these. In MUDs and MMOs it usually means a private per-party copy, and GoMud already uses it for saved room changes ("instance" YAML) and has "ephemeral" copied rooms. Use **dense layer** or **anchored area**. Private per-party copies can be a separate option later (for dungeons).

## 4. Explore

### What it is

`explore` in an overworld cell loads that cell's generated dense layer: a finer grid with resources to gather and things to fight. It's shared (anyone can visit) and is taken down after a period with no visitors. The concept comes from Calabe's Age of Legends.

### Grid depth

Sub-squares are S2 children of the overworld cell, so the dense layer reuses the same key scheme. They sample GIS data at near-native resolution: the overworld shows the summary, the explored layer shows the ground.

| Depth below overworld | Grid | Square size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| +2 (L17) | 4×4 | ~70 m | Max separation 3 squares (~210 m) |
| +3 (L18) | 8×8 | ~35 m | Max separation 7 squares (~245 m) |
| **+4 (L19)** | **16×16** | **~17.6 m** | **Leaning for a real battle grid.** Max separation 15 squares (~264 m) |

Depth is a per-project setting, and could later be set per biome or per layer type (dense forest at +4, open plain at +3, a hand-built town at whatever its builder chose).

### Entry

- `explore` enters at the **centre** of the grid, regardless of arrival side (as in Age of Legends).
- Encounters place sides as described in section 5.
- The entry point is a per-project policy hook, told the entry reason and the arrival side.

### Exit

- You leave by walking off an edge square, or through a defined exit for authored content.
- **Walking off an edge lands you in the overworld neighbour cell in that direction** (east edge to the cell east), never back in the parent cell. Same promise as a city gate.
- From a corner square the direction walked picks the cell: from the NE corner, `ne` goes to the cell northeast, `n` to the cell north, `e` to the cell east. Inner squares never exit.
- You land in the neighbour's overworld room, not its dense layer.
- "The cell to the east" means the neighbour whose centre has the nearest bearing to east (matters across cube face edges).
- Multi-cell areas follow the same rule from the edge of their footprint.

### Lifecycle and the farming trap

- A layer unloads after N minutes with no visitors and no active state (a fight, a fire).
- **Unloading drops it from memory; it does not reset it.** If teardown discarded state and re-exploring generated a fresh layer, players could harvest, wait out the teardown and explore again to farm resets.
- Layout, resource nodes and spawn tables are deterministic from cell key, seed and T, so the same meadow appears every time.
- Harvested nodes, killed mobs and dropped items are deltas with regrowth or decay rules, stored in the database. A week later the berries have regrown on schedule; ten minutes later they're still picked.
- Sub-squares become live room objects only when entered, seen into, or holding state. A fight touching 20 squares makes about 20 rooms, not 256.

## 5. Encounters

### Trigger

Entering an overworld cell can roll for an encounter. A hit loads that cell's dense layer and places the encounter in it. The ruleset owns the roll and the encounter tables; the engine provides "enter dense layer, at this point, for this reason".

### Placement by perception (Calabe's hybrid)

- Whoever **perceives the other first** chooses the spacing that suits their strengths: a bow user starts at distance, a melee fighter close in near the centre.
- **Ties** on perception give neutral placement.
- **Group vs group** encounters give neutral placement.
- The whole policy is configurable per project.
- The spacing choice is resolved **deterministically by the engine**: a preset set by the player (Diku-style, like `wimpy`) or by the NPC's creator, otherwise derived from signals such as loadout. No interactive prompt mid-ambush, which would break the twitchy feel. Determinism also makes encounters testable and replayable.
- A possible extension: winning perception could also offer slipping away unseen. Ruleset choice.

### Engine and ruleset split

| Ruleset owns | Engine owns |
|---|---|
| The perception contest and what counts as a tie | Encounter object: sides, perception winner, requested spacing, entry reason |
| Mapping signals (loadout, stance, talents) to a spacing preference | Placement solver: given spacing (close, medium, far, neutral) and approach direction, place each side on squares the terrain allows |
| How groups resolve (neutral now; "best scout" possible later) | Distance and line of sight between sub-squares, with a per-square cover value from land cover and seeded features |
| Range bands per weapon, movement per round | Combat across several sub-rooms, closing and opening distance during a fight |

### Fit with Diku timing

GoMud's round is 4 seconds. At ~18 m squares, one square per round is a natural move speed (a jog), with a sprint covering two. Bow ranges of roughly 50 to 150 m are 3 to 9 squares, so an archer who wins perception gets real volleys before contact. (Movement and range figures are estimates.)

### New engine work this implies

Upstream GoMud's `shoot` reaches one exit away only. Multi-square ranges, line of sight and moving during a fight across sub-rooms are new.

## 6. Engine versus project configuration

| Knob | Engine provides | Project sets |
|---|---|---|
| Grid implementation | Cube-sphere, flat, (equal-angle) | Which one |
| Overworld level | Any S2 level | ~L15 (leaning) |
| Planet rotation | Rotation applied before lookups | Value, fixed forever |
| Explore depth | Any depth | +4 for battle grids (leaning); per biome later |
| Explore entry point | Policy hook with entry reason and arrival side | Centre for `explore` (leaning) |
| Encounter placement | Encounter object, placement solver, deterministic resolution | Perception rules, spacing presets, tie and group rules |
| Layer teardown | Unload on idle, persist deltas | Idle timeout, decay and regrowth rules |
| Encounter rolls | Hook on overworld cell entry | Frequency, tables |

## 7. Open questions for the PRD

**Carried from the proposal**
- Base codebase: upstream GoMud (recommended in `engine_proposal.md`) or another option. Not decided.

**Grid**
- Confirm overworld S2 level 15.
- Choose the planet rotation that puts all eight cube corners in ocean (verify against a real coastline dataset, not general geography).
- Flat-grid implementation details for small and fictional worlds.
- Vertical structure on the overworld (caves, cliffs, mountains): z-levels per cell, or only inside dense layers?
- Do diagonal moves cost more (√2 of the distance)?

**Data**
- Source datasets, bake tile format, and the coarse global fallback.
- Fingerprint tests for generator drift.

**Dense layers and explore**
- Default idle timeout before teardown.
- Decay and regrowth rules for deltas in explored layers.
- What `explore` does in a cell whose dense layer is an anchored area (enter the area; anything else?).
- What happens when a second party arrives at a layer mid-fight.

**Encounters and combat**
- Range and line-of-sight model in detail: what is engine geometry, what is ruleset.
- Does an encounter roll happen in cells with anchored areas (towns)?

**Travel**
- Design of the long-distance travel primitive: routes, time passing, interruption.
